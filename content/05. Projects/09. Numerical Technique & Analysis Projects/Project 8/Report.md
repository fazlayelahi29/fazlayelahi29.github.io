## 6.4 FORMULAS

The statistical variance of the input vector is defined by the standard deviation formula:

$$ \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (x_i - \mu)^2} $$

  

The fundamental system of linear equations is universally represented as:

$$ A \cdot x = b $$

  

The resolution of the state vector array requires the strict inversion of the coefficient matrix:

$$ x = A^{-1} \cdot b $$

  

The vertical displacement in a constant-acceleration field is defined by the kinematic differential limit:

$$ y = x_{dist} \tan(\theta_0) - \frac{g \cdot x_{dist}^2}{2(v_0 \cos(\theta_0))^2} $$

  

Euler's identity governing the complex unit circle contour:

$$ z = e^{i\theta} = \cos(\theta) + i\sin(\theta) $$

  

The subsequent second-order complex transformation mapping:

$$ f(z) = z^2 = (\cos(\theta) + i\sin(\theta))^2 = \cos^2(\theta) - \sin^2(\theta) + i(2\sin(\theta)\cos(\theta)) $$

  

The magnitude constraint of the complex vector space:

$$ |z| = \sqrt{Re(z)^2 + Im(z)^2} = 1 $$

## 6.8 DERIVATION OF FORMULAS, LAWS, THEOREMS, AND PRINCIPLES

a. The derivation of the kinematic trajectory equation begins with the parametric functions of time:

$$ x_{dist}(t) = v_0 \cos(\theta_0) t $$

$$ y(t) = v_0 \sin(\theta_0) t - \frac{1}{2} g t^2 $$

b. To eliminate the temporal variable $t$, the horizontal equation is algebraically manipulated to isolate $t$:

$$ t = \frac{x_{dist}}{v_0 \cos(\theta_0)} $$

c. This derived temporal parameter is forcefully substituted back into the vertical displacement equation:

$$ y(x) = v_0 \sin(\theta_0) \left( \frac{x_{dist}}{v_0 \cos(\theta_0)} \right) - \frac{1}{2} g \left( \frac{x_{dist}}{v_0 \cos(\theta_0)} \right)^2 $$

d. By canceling the initial velocity terms in the first segment and substituting the trigonometric tangent identity, the final rigorous formula is extracted:

$$ y(x) = x_{dist} \tan(\theta_0) - \frac{g \cdot x_{dist}^2}{2v_0^2 \cos^2(\theta_0)} $$
