
## Project 1: Sinusoidal Steady-State Phasor and Phase Shift Verification

- The Circuit Structure: Design an AC circuit loop containing a sinusoidal voltage source configured with an amplitude of $10\text{V}$, a frequency of $1\text{kHz}$, and a starting phase angle of $30^\circ$. Connect this source to a series arrangement of a resistor ($R_1 = 150\Omega$), an inductor ($L_1 = 22\text{mH}$), and a capacitor ($C_1 = 2.2\mu\text{F}$).
- Mathematical Hand Calculations: Calculate the inductive reactance ($X_L = 2\pi fL$) and capacitive reactance ($X_C = 1 / (2\pi fC)$) at $1\text{kHz}$. Sum these values as complex numbers to find the total network impedance ($Z = R + j(X_L - X_C)$) in polar form. Use Ohm's Law in the phasor domain ($\mathbf{I} = \mathbf{V}/Z$) to calculate the analytical magnitude and phase angle of the total current.
- What You Will Do in LTspice: Set the AC properties of your voltage source to `Magnitude: 10` and `Phase: 30`. Place the directive `.ac dec 1 1kHz 1kHz` to execute a single frequency evaluation. Run the simulation. Open the SPICE error log (`Ctrl+L`) to view the output data computed in polar coordinates.
- What to Document in Your Google Doc: Copy and paste the raw text lines from the SPICE error log containing the complex data array. Create a detailed text guide teaching readers how to interpret the comma-separated polar outputs (Magnitude $\text{dB}$ and Phase Angle in degrees) and map them directly back to handwritten complex numbers.

- Mathematical Hand Calculations: Calculate the theoretical resonant center frequency using the formula $f_0 = 1 / (2\pi \sqrt{LC})$. Compute the series Quality Factor ($Q = (1/R)\sqrt{L/C}$) and the half-power bandwidth ($\mathcal{B} = f_0 / Q$).

- Mathematical Hand Calculations: Calculate the complex apparent power ($\mathbf{S} = P + jQ$) drawn by the inductive load network. Find the real power ($P$ in Watts), the reactive power ($Q$ in VAR), and the baseline power factor ($\text{PF} = \cos(\theta)$). Calculate the exact value of a parallel compensation capacitor ($C_{\text{comp}}$) required to offset $Q$ and pull the system power factor up to an ideal $1.0$.

Use `Ctrl+Click` on the plotted power trace label to have LTspice calculate the mathematical average (Real Power). Now, connect your calculated $C_{\text{comp}}$ capacitor directly in parallel with the load and re-run the trace.
- What to Document in Your Google Doc: Show side-by-side plots of the total grid current waveform before and after adding the parallel capacitor.
